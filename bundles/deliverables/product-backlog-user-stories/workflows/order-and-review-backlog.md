# Order and Review a Product Backlog

1. Confirm the product boundary, Product Goal, source-of-record backlog, and review date/version.
2. Inventory the candidate items and label evidence status for each item.
3. Check that the backlog is one transparent list for the product when the team is using Scrum; do not infer the team's operating model.
4. Group items by outcome, risk, dependency, learning need, or other stated decision dimension without hiding the original item identity.
5. Choose an ordering basis. If using RICE, MoSCoW, WSJF, or another method, record the definition, inputs, scope, time period, owner, and uncertainty. If inputs are missing, do not calculate a score.
6. Surface dependencies, sequencing constraints, risk reduction, learning value, urgency, and unresolved trade-offs.
7. Check refinement: description, order, size, decomposition, acceptance evidence, and readiness for the stated planning boundary.
8. Produce the proposed order, rationale, confidence, and what evidence could change it.
9. Ask the accountable product decision-maker and delivery team to confirm the order and sizing convention.
10. Show the exact proposed changes and obtain explicit confirmation before writing to a live system.

## Direct-Answer Pattern

Start with the proposed order and the strongest reason. Then define what each item measures, state evidence and scope, list expected differences or trade-offs, and separate unresolved discrepancies from conclusions.

## Source Note

The Scrum Guide grounds Product Backlog, Product Goal, ordering, refinement, and sizing. Scrum.org provides supporting explanations of transparency, ordering factors, and complementary user-story practice. Team-specific policy and product evidence must be inspected.
