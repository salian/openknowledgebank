# Evidence-to-Filing-Readiness Workflow

1. **Fix the scope.** Confirm the taxpayer-approved identifier, filing population, federal jurisdiction, tax year, return type, and excluded state, local, foreign, or entity obligations.
2. **Inspect current authority.** Retrieve the applicable IRS form, instructions, schedules, post-release changes, due-date or relief guidance, and filing method. Record the revision and access date.
3. **Inventory evidence.** Register each supplied source document, period, issuer, recipient, amount class, completeness state, and handling restriction. Record missing expected evidence without inventing it.
4. **Map applicability.** Link each proposed form, schedule, attachment, credit, deduction, election, payment, or carryover to a current instruction and taxpayer evidence. Mark unsupported items `Needs verification`.
5. **Reconcile.** Align conflicting values by taxpayer, tax year, period, source scope, gross/net treatment, timing, entity, identifiers, adjustments, aggregation, and duplication. Preserve unresolved differences.
6. **Prepare the draft.** Produce the [return package](deliverable.md) with evidence references and calculation support. Keep all outputs clearly marked draft.
7. **Review exceptions.** Have the accountable reviewer examine missing evidence, uncertain positions, elections, material estimates, reconciliations, and privacy handling. An unevidenced reviewer remains `Needs verification`.
8. **Separate authorization from preparation.** Record taxpayer review and signature authorization only from evidence. Do not infer consent from document delivery or draft approval.
9. **Separate transmission from acceptance.** If an approved filing system is used after explicit authorization, retain its transmission and authority acknowledgement or rejection evidence. Do not describe a draft or transmission attempt as an accepted filing.
10. **Close and retain.** Record the filed copy, acknowledgement, payment evidence, unresolved follow-up, and the current authority used for each retention decision.

## Inspect-First Checklist

- Applicable tax year and filing population
- Current form revision, instructions, schedules, and post-release changes
- Current due date, extension, relief, and payment rules
- Taxpayer-provided facts and source-document completeness
- Preparer/reviewer authority and taxpayer authorization
- Filing-system acknowledgement or rejection evidence

## Source Note

The workflow is grounded in current IRS filing, Form 1040, recordkeeping, and e-file authorization guidance. It does not contain local taxpayer evidence. Every taxpayer-specific conclusion, exact form treatment, date, authorization, payment, and filing state requires current official guidance and supplied evidence.
