# Risk Management Policy / Framework Source-Aware Guide

Use this guide to draft a reviewable policy or framework from supplied organizational evidence. It is a decision aid, not proof of compliance, certification, implementation, or authorization.

## Evidence Required

- Policy purpose, scope, entities, activities, exclusions, owner, effective date, and approval authority
- Applicable jurisdiction, regulator, law, contract, license, standard, and source version
- Strategy, objectives, planning horizon, material risks, and relevant capital or operating constraints
- Risk taxonomy, definitions, assessment method, appetite/capacity/limits, metrics, data sources, and thresholds
- Governance charters, delegated authority, RACI, escalation routes, exceptions, review cadence, and assurance responsibilities
- Evidence for current controls, issues, incidents, risk reports, approvals, and change history

## Application Sequence

1. Define the decision, scope, entity, audience, and adoption boundary.
2. Inventory source documents and local evidence; label each item `Verified`, `Provided`, `Assumed`, or `Needs verification`.
3. Reconcile jurisdiction, entity, period, version, definition, metric, scope, owner, and approval conflicts.
4. Draft the policy using the architecture in [Policy Architecture](frameworks/policy-architecture.md).
5. Leave organization-specific values as placeholders or missing evidence until inspected and approved.
6. Review the draft with accountable risk, compliance, legal, finance, internal audit, and management stakeholders as applicable.
7. Obtain explicit approval before adoption, limit changes, risk acceptance, regulatory representation, or disclosure.

## Required Policy Sections

- Document control and approval record
- Purpose, scope, applicability, and exclusions
- Principles and relationship to strategy and performance
- Governance, accountabilities, independence, and delegated authority
- Risk taxonomy and assessment language
- Risk appetite, capacity, limits, tolerances, triggers, and escalation
- Identification, assessment, treatment, monitoring, reporting, and issue management
- Exceptions, incidents, breaches, records, confidentiality, and retention
- Review, testing, assurance, change control, and version history
- Source note, evidence gaps, unresolved decisions, and approval boundary

## Guardrails

- ISO 31000 is guidance and must not be described as a certification scheme.
- FSB and Basel concepts are especially relevant to financial institutions; applicability must be checked.
- Never invent thresholds, owners, committees, control effectiveness, risk ratings, incidents, or compliance status.
- Require explicit confirmation before accepting risk, changing a control or limit, making a regulatory claim, or publishing sensitive risk information.
