# Security Policy Lifecycle

1. Define the decision, audience, scope, systems, data, jurisdictions, and obligations.
2. Inspect selected official sources and record edition, access date, and relevant source surfaces.
3. Separate **Verified**, **Provided**, **Assumed**, and **Needs verification** facts.
4. Decide whether the artifact is a policy, standard, procedure, or guideline; do not combine layers silently.
5. Draft purpose, scope, definitions, requirements, roles, evidence, exceptions, enforcement, review, and change control.
6. Build a source-linked control map with applicability, evidence, gaps, compensating measures, and unresolved questions.
7. Submit to the accountable owner and approval authority. Do not infer either from a generic role.
8. Record exceptions with rationale, risk owner, compensating measure, expiry, review date, and approval evidence when supplied.
9. Publish or enforce only after explicit approval and confirmation of distribution and implementation mechanisms.
10. Review on the documented cadence and after material changes, using current sources and evidence.

## Visible Output Contract

Include a short **Source note**, evidence status, open questions, approval status, and **Needs verification** list. If no local evidence is supplied, set Verified, Provided, and Assumed to None and do not nominate an owner or approver.
