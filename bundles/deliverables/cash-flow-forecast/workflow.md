# Cash Flow Forecast Workflow

1. **Define the decision and perimeter.** Identify the question, entity, accounts, currencies, cash definition, start date, horizon, cadence, cut-off, materiality, users, and review authority. Do not default to a 13-week or monthly model without context.
2. **Reconcile opening cash.** Tie each included balance to current source evidence and timestamp. Identify restricted amounts, unreconciled items, overdrafts, excluded accounts, and internal-transfer boundaries.
3. **Inventory receipts and payments.** Gather dated or driver-based evidence for customer receipts, suppliers, payroll, tax, capital, financing, investment, intercompany, and one-off flows. Preserve source and owner.
4. **Profile timing.** Use due dates, calendars, contractual terms, historical behavior, operating drivers, and known events. Mark unsupported amount or timing as an assumption; avoid unexplained even spreading.
5. **Build the base case.** Roll opening cash through receipts, payments, eliminations, and supported reconciling effects to ending cash for every period. Check signs, subtotals, currencies, and continuity.
6. **Assess liquidity and thresholds.** Show unrestricted cash and evidence-backed facility availability separately. Calculate minimum-cash, liquidity, and covenant headroom only when definitions and source terms are supplied.
7. **Model uncertainty.** Change named drivers for supported scenarios, preserve dependencies, identify threshold crossings, and keep proposed mitigations separate from the base case until approved.
8. **Reconcile integrated forecasts.** Where projected profit and loss and balance sheet data exist, align working-capital timing, non-cash items, capital, financing, and ending cash. Record, rather than plug, unexplained differences.
9. **Compare with actuals.** Freeze the forecast version, load reconciled actual cash, classify amount and timing variance consistently, identify root causes, and update future assumptions without rewriting history.
10. **Review and communicate.** State the direct liquidity conclusion, lowest point, headroom, uncertainty, key drivers, gaps, risks, decisions needed, owners, and review status. Keep approval and execution separate.

## Review Questions

- Does opening cash reconcile to a timestamped source?
- Can each material receipt and payment be traced to evidence or an explicit assumption?
- Are timing, currency, internal transfers, restrictions, facilities, and thresholds handled consistently?
- Do ending balances roll into the next period without plugs?
- Are scenarios driver-based and are mitigations excluded until approved?
- Can forecast-versus-actual results be reproduced from frozen versions and consistent cut-offs?
- Are liquidity, solvency, covenant, approval, and execution claims appropriately qualified?

## Current-Source Check

Verify the applicable accounting framework, treasury policy, facility and covenant documents, bank and ledger sources, and current operating assumptions before relying on exact classifications or decisions. External guidance does not replace organization-specific evidence.
