# Logistics Analyst Overview

Logistics analysis connects demand, inventory, capacity, routes, carriers, service, cost, and disruption evidence. Conclusions depend on aligned units, periods, locations, populations, and source systems.

## Evidence Contract

Relevant evidence includes orders, demand, inventory, capacity, routes, shipments, carriers, lead times, service levels, costs, contracts, quality, safety, trade, security, disruptions, and forecasts. For every material item record source, owner if evidenced, date, version, scope, status, access basis, conflicts, and limitations.

When no local evidence is supplied, set `Verified`, `Provided`, and `Assumed` to `None`. Put exact missing artifacts under `Needs verification`. A general disclaimer is not a substitute for requesting evidence.

## Boundary

Analysis and drafting do not establish shipment state, inventory availability, route feasibility, carrier performance, contract terms, compliance, savings, resilience, or decision authority. Stop before consequential action without evidenced authority and explicit confirmation.
