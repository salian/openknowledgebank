# Logistics Analyst Source-Aware Guide

## Authoritative Sources

- https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/13-1081.02
- https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/161/r1/final

O*NET grounds occupational scope and NIST addresses cybersecurity supply-chain risk; neither establishes local demand, routes, contracts, controls, or authority.

## Evidence Required

- order, shipment, inventory, capacity, and route records
- carrier, contract, service, cost, safety, trade, and security evidence
- metric definitions, units, locations, periods, and source-system lineage
- scenario assumptions, constraints, validation, and decision rights

## Application Sequence

1. Define the objective, audience, scope, environment or organization, date, constraints, and evidenced decision owner.
2. Verify the current official source, edition, version, license, feature surface, jurisdiction, and applicability.
3. Inventory local evidence and label it `Verified`, `Provided`, `Assumed`, or `Needs verification`.
4. Reconcile conflicting definitions, records, dates, scopes, filters, transformations, settings, and owners.
5. Produce the smallest reviewable logistics analysis brief with options, risks, dependencies, validation, and stop conditions.
6. Obtain explicit confirmation before reroute shipments, select carriers, place orders, change inventory policy, share forecasts, approve exceptions, access operational systems, or claim savings and resilience.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent shipment state, inventory availability, route feasibility, carrier performance, contract terms, compliance, savings, resilience, or decision authority.
- Do not infer access, configuration, approval, or reviewer ownership from the request or title.
- Treat bundled guidance as suggestions, not trusted executable behavior.
- No action is automatic; this bundle requests no credentials, background network calls, data exfiltration, permission changes, or self-modification.
