# Overview

Group Product Manager and Director of Product are related but non-interchangeable product-leadership archetypes. Public employer and practitioner frameworks often position a GPM closer to a product area and PM coaching, while a Director more often spans multiple groups, develops product leaders, and shapes a broader operating model. Titles, management layers, and authority differ substantially between organizations.

## Archetype matrix

| Dimension | GPM hypothesis | Director hypothesis | Verify locally |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary scope | related product area or group | multiple groups, portfolio segment, or product function | product taxonomy and charter |
| Leadership mode | player-coach, PM manager, or area lead | manager-of-managers, senior people leader, or portfolio lead | reporting lines and management accountabilities |
| Work emphasis | area strategy, team coaching, cross-team coherence | portfolio coherence, leadership systems, organizational capability | approved role framework |
| Decision horizon | broader than one team | broader and often longer-range | planning and governance records |
| Interfaces | product, engineering, design, go-to-market partners | functional leaders and senior decision forums | stakeholder and decision map |

The table is a discovery aid, not a leveling rule. A GPM can be an individual contributor, a Director can directly manage PMs, and either title can carry narrower or broader scope.

## Evidence states

Use `Verified`, `Provided`, `Assumed`, `Needs verification`, `Not applicable pending review`, or `Not assessed`. With no local evidence, mark title level, product scope, reporting line, direct reports, staffing, budget, access, authority, metrics, targets, and reviewer as `Needs verification`.

## Sources and boundaries

GitLab's current leadership framework separates Group Manager and Director scope; Intercom describes scope, autonomy, and impact as contextual career dimensions; Redgate publishes a company-specific GPM model. These sources support archetypes only. Their direct-report counts, grades, commercial duties, metrics, planning horizons, or P&L language do not transfer to another organization.
