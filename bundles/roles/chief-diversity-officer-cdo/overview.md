# Overview

Chief Diversity Officer (CDO) is a role archetype whose mandate varies by organization, level, industry, jurisdiction, operating model, and local charter. Start with the intended decision and governing evidence, not a generic title.

## Role boundaries

Separate executive sponsorship, HR ownership, equal-opportunity and legal review, privacy and data stewardship, workforce analytics, employee representation, line-management execution, and independent review. Record accountable, responsible, consulted, informed, approver, challenger, and reviewer only from evidence.

## Evidence contract

Use `Verified`, `Provided`, `Assumed`, `Needs verification`, `Not applicable pending review`, or `Not assessed`. Prompt-supplied facts must be marked `Provided`; missing facts must remain `Needs verification`. Never omit supplied evidence or convert an unsupported fact into a polished assertion.

Define populations, denominators, comparison groups, time windows, suppression rules, uncertainty, intersectional limits, adverse-impact review, owners, and approved uses before interpreting disparities.

Sources: https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/11-1011.00; https://www.eeoc.gov/employers/summary-selected-recordkeeping-obligations-29-cfr-part-1602. O*NET lists Chief Diversity Officer as an executive title and EEOC guidance addresses U.S. federal requirements; neither establishes local mandate, lawful data use, causation, discrimination, reporting duty, or intervention effectiveness.
