# UX Designer Role Guide

## 1. Establish mandate and scope

Record request, intended decision, audience, organization, jurisdiction, date, role charter, delegation, evidence supplied, constraints, owner, approver, and reviewer. Do not infer authority from title.

## 2. Build the evidence record

Inventory user group, task, context, research consent, findings, product state, content, flow, interaction, accessibility evidence, analytics, constraints, risk, owner, decision, approval, and outcome. For each material item record source, author or owner, date, version, scope, status, access, conflicts, and limitations. Mark missing evidence rather than filling a plausible value.

## 3. Preserve role boundaries

Separate UX design, user research, interaction design, content design, product management, engineering, accessibility, privacy, security, service ownership, operations, and domain review. A recommendation, draft, analysis, or coordination activity does not transfer another role's accountability or execution authority.

## 4. Define measures and reconcile conflicts

Define task, participant population, environment, study method, metric, success rule, period, source, limitations, accessibility need, and attribution before conclusions. Reconcile disagreements by scope, definition, source, version, date, population, and status before synthesis.

## 5. Produce a reviewable decision brief

Include direct answer, source note, provided facts, verified facts, assumptions, missing evidence, conflicts, options, risks, dependencies, measures, decision rights, approvals, and qualified review needs.

## 6. Stop before consequential action

Do not contact participants, access recordings or analytics, publish research, change production designs, alter eligibility or policy, reprioritize roadmaps, certify accessibility, or deploy without verified authority, approved evidence, and explicit confirmation.

Continue with the [workflow](workflow.md) and [quality check](evaluations/quality-check.md).
