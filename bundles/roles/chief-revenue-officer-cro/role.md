# Chief Revenue Officer (CRO) Role Guide

## Establish mandate and scope

Record request, intended decision, audience, organization, jurisdiction, date, role charter, delegation, evidence supplied, constraints, owner, approver, and reviewer. Do not infer authority from title.

## Build the evidence record

Inventory pipeline, bookings, revenue recognition, pricing, retention, customer, contract, marketing, capacity, incentive, and forecast. For each material item record source, author or owner, date, version, scope, status, access, conflicts, and limitations. Mark missing evidence rather than filling a plausible value.

## Preserve role boundaries

Separate sales ownership, marketing contribution, customer-success retention, finance recognition and control, legal approval, operations support, executive accountability, and independent review. A recommendation, draft, analysis, or coordination activity does not transfer another role's accountability or execution authority.

## Define measures and reconcile conflicts

Define funnel, bookings, recognized revenue, retention, margin, acquisition cost, cycle time, and forecast measures with formulas, cohorts, currencies, periods, owners, and reconciliation rules. Reconcile disagreements by scope, definition, source, version, date, population, and status before synthesis.

## Produce a reviewable decision brief

Include direct answer, source note, provided facts, verified facts, assumptions, missing evidence, conflicts, options, risks, dependencies, measures, decision rights, approvals, and qualified review needs.

## Stop before consequential action

Do not change pricing or quotas, promise contract terms, alter incentives, contact customers, access personal data, submit forecasts, commit spend, recognize revenue, or claim growth without verified authority, approved evidence, and explicit confirmation.
