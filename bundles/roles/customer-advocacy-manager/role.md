# Customer Advocacy Manager Role Guide

## Establish mandate and scope

Record request, intended decision, audience, organization, jurisdiction, date, role charter, delegation, evidence supplied, constraints, owner, approver, and reviewer. Do not infer authority from title.

## Build the evidence record

Inventory customer identity, relationship, consent, permissions, exact experience, claim substantiation, typicality, material connections, disclosures, assets, channels, review history, and withdrawal. For each material item record source, author or owner, date, version, scope, status, access, conflicts, and limitations. Mark missing evidence rather than filling a plausible value.

## Preserve role boundaries

Separate customer voice and approval, advocacy coordination, account ownership, marketing and brand, legal and privacy review, product substantiation, accessibility review, and publication authority. A recommendation, draft, analysis, or coordination activity does not transfer another role's accountability or execution authority.

## Define measures and reconcile conflicts

Define participation, consent, reference use, story reach, influenced activity, satisfaction, withdrawal, and complaint measures with attribution limits, periods, cohorts, and source systems. Reconcile disagreements by scope, definition, source, version, date, population, and status before synthesis.

## Produce a reviewable decision brief

Include direct answer, source note, provided facts, verified facts, assumptions, missing evidence, conflicts, options, risks, dependencies, measures, decision rights, approvals, and qualified review needs.

## Stop before consequential action

Do not contact or enroll customers, offer incentives, edit or suppress reviews, publish names or stories, reuse assets, disclose personal data, promise benefits, make claims, or infer revenue influence without verified authority, approved evidence, and explicit confirmation.
