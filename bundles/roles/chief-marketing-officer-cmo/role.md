# Chief Marketing Officer (CMO) Role Guide

## 1. Establish mandate and scope

Record request, intended decision, audience, organization, jurisdiction, date, role charter, delegation, evidence supplied, constraints, owner, approver, and reviewer. Do not infer authority from title.

## 2. Build the evidence record

Inventory market, audience, research, brand, offer, product capability, substantiation, price, channel, consent, spend, attribution, revenue, target, owner, approval, and outcome. For each material item record source, author or owner, date, version, scope, status, access, conflicts, and limitations. Mark missing evidence rather than filling a plausible value.

## 3. Preserve role boundaries

Separate board and CEO oversight, product and commercial ownership, marketing strategy, brand, product marketing, demand generation, sales, finance, privacy, legal review, and agency execution. A recommendation, draft, analysis, or coordination activity does not transfer another role's accountability or execution authority.

## 4. Define measures and reconcile conflicts

Define spend, reach, response, lead, pipeline, revenue, incrementality, attribution, period, population, currency, source, exclusions, and uncertainty before comparison. Reconcile disagreements by scope, definition, source, version, date, population, and status before synthesis.

## 5. Produce a reviewable decision brief

Include direct answer, source note, provided facts, verified facts, assumptions, missing evidence, conflicts, options, risks, dependencies, measures, decision rights, approvals, and qualified review needs.

## 6. Stop before consequential action

Do not publish claims, launch or pause campaigns, buy media, change pricing, contact audiences, share customer data, commit budget, direct agencies, or make public statements without verified authority, approved evidence, and explicit confirmation.
