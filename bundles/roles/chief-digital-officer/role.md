# Chief Digital Officer Role Guide

## Establish mandate and scope

Record request, intended decision, audience, organization, jurisdiction, date, role charter, delegation, evidence supplied, constraints, owner, approver, and reviewer. Do not infer authority from title.

## Build the evidence record

Inventory strategy, user need, service and process baseline, product portfolio, technology and data, accessibility, privacy, security, workforce, supplier, cost, benefit, dependency, risk, owner, approval, implementation, and outcome. For each material item record source, author or owner, date, version, scope, status, access, conflicts, and limitations. Mark missing evidence rather than filling a plausible value.

## Preserve role boundaries

Separate board and CEO oversight, chief digital officer, CIO, CTO, CDO, product and service owners, operations, design, engineering, data, security, privacy, finance, HR, procurement, change leadership, and assurance. A recommendation, draft, analysis, or coordination activity does not transfer another role's accountability or execution authority.

## Define measures and reconcile conflicts

Define service, user population, channel, process, baseline, benefit, cost, period, source, target, attribution, dependency, quality, and owner before transformation claims. Reconcile disagreements by scope, definition, source, version, date, population, and status before synthesis.

## Produce a reviewable decision brief

Include direct answer, source note, provided facts, verified facts, assumptions, missing evidence, conflicts, options, risks, dependencies, measures, decision rights, approvals, and qualified review needs.

## Stop before consequential action

Do not change policy or operating processes, reprioritize products, deploy technology, access data, restructure teams, instruct suppliers, commit funding, publish services, or claim realized benefits without verified authority, approved evidence, and explicit confirmation.
