# Product Operations Manager Role Guide

## 1. Define the charter

Record the problem the function exists to solve, internal customers, outcomes, service catalog, exclusions, interfaces, decision rights, escalation paths, staffing model, and review cadence. Distinguish the manager title from actual people-management responsibility.

Choose and adapt evidenced archetypes: product-system and process enablement; insights and feedback operations; tooling, data, and knowledge administration; cross-functional planning or launch cadence; PM onboarding, training, and change enablement. A role may combine them, but every component needs an owner and boundary.

## 2. Map services and decisions

For each service, record requester, user, trigger, inputs, workflow, system of record, accountable decision owner, consultation, output, service expectation, control, and escalation. Separate process administration, analysis, facilitation, recommendation, approval, and execution.

Do not infer that Product Operations owns product strategy, roadmap priority, discovery, customer research, analytics definitions, launch approval, experimentation, product quality, engineering delivery, or go-to-market outcomes. Map overlap with Product Management, Engineering, Design, Research, Data, Marketing, Sales, Customer Success, Support, Finance, Legal, Security, and IT using local evidence.

## 3. Review systems and access

Inventory tools only from provided records. For each system, record purpose, system owner, administrator, data owner, user groups, access level, integration, source of truth, retention, sensitivity, and change approval. Access to a report does not imply permission to query underlying data or modify configuration.

Identify duplicate entry, unclear source of truth, broken handoff, inaccessible knowledge, inconsistent taxonomy, and manual work as observed issues. Do not change a workflow or automate work until authority, controls, failure handling, privacy, security, and rollback are confirmed.

## 4. Define measures without inventing them

Possible measurement themes include flow efficiency, feedback latency, data quality, process adoption, tool usefulness, launch readiness, PM administrative load, stakeholder service, and decision support. These are prompts, not assigned metrics or targets.

For every measure, record question, owner, formula, numerator, denominator, unit, grain, population, time period, filters, exclusions, source, refresh, target, quality limits, and interpretation. When two values differ, neither is automatically correct: align definitions, inputs, timing, identity, aggregation, deduplication, and processing before explaining expected and unresolved differences.

Do not use team velocity, feature adoption, revenue, customer satisfaction, or launch timing as Product Operations performance measures without a documented causal theory and accountable approval.

## 5. Produce evidence-first deliverables

Every charter, cadence map, feedback review, or reporting brief should include a source note naming practitioner context, local evidence used, and missing evidence. Label observations, hypotheses, decisions, and recommendations separately. Record the accountable reviewer as `Needs verification` until provided.

Protect restricted data and stop before system changes, bulk exports, customer contact, roadmap publication, metric certification, vendor procurement, access changes, or organizational commitments. Continue with the [workflow](workflow.md) and [quality check](evaluations/quality-check.md).
