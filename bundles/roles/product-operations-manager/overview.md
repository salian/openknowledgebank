# Overview

Product Operations helps a product organization operate more consistently and make better-supported decisions. Practitioner sources commonly describe systems and process enablement, insights and feedback operations, tooling and knowledge administration, cross-functional cadence, and PM onboarding or enablement. Each is an archetype to validate, not a universal mandate.

## Archetype selection

A function may be centralized, embedded with product teams, shared across existing roles, or led by a manager with people responsibilities. Record the organization's reason for the function, internal customers, expected outcomes, services, exclusions, interfaces, and authority. Do not infer maturity or need from team size alone.

## Evidence states

Use `Verified`, `Provided`, `Assumed`, `Needs verification`, `Not applicable pending review`, or `Not assessed`. With no local evidence, set applicant, organization, access, system, metric, ownership, and reviewer facts to `Needs verification`; do not populate a plausible stack or charter.

## Boundaries

Product Operations can administer a roadmap process without deciding roadmap priority, publish a dashboard without owning its metric, coordinate a launch without approving release, and aggregate feedback without contacting customers or choosing what to build. See the [role guide](role.md) and [workflow](workflow.md).
