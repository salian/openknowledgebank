# Source-Aware Workflow

1. Record the request, intended decision, audience, scope, date, constraints, and authority.
2. Verify current occupational and professional sources, jurisdiction, actual role, qualifications, and local procedures.
3. Inventory learner and context research, language variety and target-level rationale, descriptor-to-objective map, lesson task and feedback sequence, assessment blueprint rubric and validation, cultural and linguistic review, source and SME review, text audio image and font rights, captions transcripts alternatives and accessibility tests, platform browser and device tests, learner-data controls, pilot observations, revisions, and approvals.
4. Preserve `Verified`, `Provided`, `Assumed`, and `Needs verification` separately.
5. Define included and excluded work, records, systems, permissions, dependencies, validation, and rollback or stop conditions.
6. Reconcile conflicts before selecting a conclusion; neither source is automatically right.
7. Draft the online English curriculum and assessment map, including alternatives, risks, dependencies, owners only when evidenced, review points, and stop conditions.
8. Require explicit confirmation before taking any action to label proficiency without assessment, copy protected materials, stereotype learners, score or certify without authority, expose learner data, publish courses, claim conformance, or promise learning outcomes.

### Source Note
List authoritative sources used, local evidence used, and missing sources.
