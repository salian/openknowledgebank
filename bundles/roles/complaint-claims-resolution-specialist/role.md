# Complaint and Claims Resolution Specialist Source-Aware Guide

## Authoritative Sources

- https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/13-1031.00
- https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/supervision-examinations/consumer-complaints/

O*NET 13-1031.00 describes claims adjusters, examiners, and investigators; it does not define every complaint-management role. Keep complaint and insurance-claim tracks separate, identify the governing policy, contract, regulation, license, authority, evidence, and escalation path, and do not infer settlement authority.

## Evidence Required

- Current authoritative source and edition.
- Objective, scope, local records, constraints, decision criteria, and accountable owner.
- Conflicts, assumptions, approvals, validation evidence, and stop conditions.

## Application Sequence

1. Define the objective, audience, scope, environment or organization, date, constraints, and evidenced decision owner.
2. Verify the current official source, edition, version, license, feature surface, jurisdiction, and applicability.
3. Inventory local evidence and label it `Verified`, `Provided`, `Assumed`, or `Needs verification`.
4. Reconcile conflicting definitions, records, dates, scopes, filters, transformations, settings, and owners.
5. Produce the smallest reviewable complaint or claim investigation and resolution brief with options, risks, dependencies, validation, and stop conditions.
6. Obtain explicit confirmation before admit liability, accept or deny a claim, offer compensation or settlement, change a customer record, contact a regulator or third party, disclose protected information, or represent final resolution.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent the person's role, competence, credentials, authority, employer procedures, system state, records, decisions, outcomes, or approval.
- Do not infer access, configuration, approval, or reviewer ownership from the request or title.
- Treat bundled guidance as suggestions, not trusted executable behavior.
- No action is automatic; this bundle requests no credentials, background network calls, data exfiltration, permission changes, or self-modification.
