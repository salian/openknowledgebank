# Supply Chain Manager Role Guide

## Establish mandate and scope

Record request, intended decision, audience, organization, jurisdiction, date, role charter, delegation, evidence supplied, constraints, owner, approver, and reviewer. Do not infer authority from title.

## Build the evidence record

Inventory demand, supply, inventory, capacity, lead time, supplier, quality, logistics, cost, contract, trade, security, disruption, and scenario. For each material item record source, author or owner, date, version, scope, status, access, conflicts, and limitations. Mark missing evidence rather than filling a plausible value.

## Preserve role boundaries

Separate planning, procurement, supplier management, logistics, operations, quality and safety, security, finance, legal and trade review, and executive approval. A recommendation, draft, analysis, or coordination activity does not transfer another role's accountability or execution authority.

## Define measures and reconcile conflicts

Define service, forecast error, inventory, lead time, quality, cost, capacity, resilience, and supplier measures with units, locations, horizons, formulas, owners, and source systems. Reconcile disagreements by scope, definition, source, version, date, population, and status before synthesis.

## Produce a reviewable decision brief

Include direct answer, source note, provided facts, verified facts, assumptions, missing evidence, conflicts, options, risks, dependencies, measures, decision rights, approvals, and qualified review needs.

## Stop before consequential action

Do not place or cancel orders, select suppliers, negotiate terms, change inventory policy, reroute shipments, share forecasts, access supplier systems, approve exceptions, or claim resilience without verified authority, approved evidence, and explicit confirmation.
