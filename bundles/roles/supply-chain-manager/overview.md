# Overview

Supply Chain Manager is a role archetype whose mandate varies by organization, level, industry, jurisdiction, operating model, and local charter. Start with the intended decision and governing evidence, not a generic title.

## Role boundaries

Separate planning, procurement, supplier management, logistics, operations, quality and safety, security, finance, legal and trade review, and executive approval. Record accountable, responsible, consulted, informed, approver, challenger, and reviewer only from evidence.

## Evidence contract

Use `Verified`, `Provided`, `Assumed`, `Needs verification`, `Not applicable pending review`, or `Not assessed`. Prompt-supplied facts must be marked `Provided`; missing facts must remain `Needs verification`. Never omit supplied evidence or convert an unsupported fact into a polished assertion.

Define service, forecast error, inventory, lead time, quality, cost, capacity, resilience, and supplier measures with units, locations, horizons, formulas, owners, and source systems.

Sources: https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/11-3071.04; https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/161/r1/final. O*NET describes occupational work and NIST SP 800-161 addresses cybersecurity supply-chain risk; neither proves demand, supplier performance, inventory, compliance, resilience, or purchasing authority.
