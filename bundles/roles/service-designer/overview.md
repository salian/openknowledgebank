# Overview

Service design examines how an end-to-end service helps a person complete a goal and how the participating organization or organizations deliver it. A current UK Government framework includes transactions, products, content, digital and offline channels, but that public-sector model does not establish another employer's role level or authority.

## Discipline and accountability boundaries

- **Service design:** frames the whole-service boundary and maps journeys, touchpoints, actors, handoffs, user-facing and backstage activity, support systems, evidence, and improvement hypotheses.
- **Interaction design:** specifies how people interact within particular flows and how elements behave. A service blueprint can reference a touchpoint without replacing its interaction specification.
- **User research:** plans, conducts, analyzes, and governs research. Service designers use and synthesize evidence without inventing findings or independently authorizing participant contact.
- **Product management:** balances value and outcomes, manages product lifecycle, and prioritizes work in the cited government framework. Service-design recommendations are not priority decisions.
- **Service ownership:** carries accountability for service quality, performance, benefits, outcomes, direction, risk, and funding in the cited government model. Service design is not that accountability.
- **Operations:** runs live processes, people, records, suppliers, controls, incidents, and support. Mapping or recommending a change does not authorize its execution.

## Evidence states

Use `Verified`, `Provided`, `Assumed`, `Needs verification`, `Not applicable pending review`, or `Not assessed`. With no local evidence, mark users, needs, policy, service boundary, actors, touchpoints, operations, systems, access, owners, metrics, targets, authority, and reviewer as `Needs verification`.

## Current and proposed service

Keep current observations, reported facts, inferred causes, proposed design, approved decisions, and implemented changes separate. A polished blueprint is not evidence that every lane is accurate, every owner agreed, or any change was funded or delivered.
