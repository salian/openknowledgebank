# Service Design Review Workflow

1. Record the request, intended audience, decision, user goal, policy or organizational intent, jurisdiction, service context, evidence supplied, and evidence date.
2. Inventory approved research, policy, service standards, journeys, blueprints, processes, products, content, operations, systems, data, metrics, access, and approval records without accessing unprovided sources.
3. Define and justify the service boundary, start and end, included channels and actors, exclusions, adjacent services, and dependencies.
4. Map the current service with user-facing touchpoints, backstage activities, support systems, information and value flows, handoffs, waits, rules, controls, failures, recovery, owners, and source evidence.
5. Reconcile conflicting maps and measures by scope, version, date, actor, channel, definition, source, and status before synthesis.
6. Separate interaction-design, user-research, product-management, service-owner, operational, policy, commercial, and service-design responsibilities; mark unsupported ownership `Needs verification`.
7. Develop proposed options as hypotheses with affected users and staff, dependencies, risks, accessibility, privacy, security, policy, operational, commercial, transition, and decision-owner needs.
8. Draft the requested map, blueprint, handoff review, or decision brief with evidence states, source note, assumptions, conflicts, measures, open questions, and accountable review needs.
9. Apply the quality check for invented lanes, unsupported causes, role overreach, missing channels, metric gaps, and consequential actions.
10. Stop before participant contact, data or system access, policy or operational change, staffing or budget commitment, procurement, supplier instruction, priority change, publication, or communication.

Use the [role guide](role.md) for controls and the [quality check](evaluations/quality-check.md) before relying on the output.
