# Service Designer Role Guide

## 1. Establish the service boundary

Record the user's goal, policy or organizational intent, start and end, included and excluded journeys, jurisdictions, channels, products, content, actors, organizations, and lifecycle stage. State the decision the work should support and who can make it.

Do not define the service only around an existing system, department, form, or requested solution. Also do not expand it indefinitely: record adjacent services and dependencies separately, with evidence for why they are inside or outside scope.

## 2. Map current service evidence

For each journey stage and touchpoint, record user goal, trigger, channel, actor, user-facing action, backstage activity, support system or record, information or value exchanged, handoff, wait or queue, business or policy rule, control, failure, recovery, owner, and evidence source.

Use `Unknown` or `Needs verification` instead of completing a plausible lane. Distinguish direct observation, participant evidence, operational record, policy or procedure, analytics, stakeholder report, and inference. Do not combine conflicting maps until scope, version, date, actor, channel, and source are reconciled.

## 3. Frame proposed change without taking authority

Separate problem evidence, design principle, hypothesis, option, dependency, risk, recommendation, decision, approval, implementation, and observed outcome. For each option, identify affected users and staff, policy and legal implications, operational and supplier dependencies, product and interaction changes, accessibility and assisted-digital needs, privacy and security, cost or capacity evidence, transition, rollback, and accountable decision owners.

Do not infer authority to change policy, eligibility, service levels, operating procedures, staffing, supplier work, product priorities, systems, records, funding, or public communications. A service designer may facilitate agreement or recommend change while another role decides and executes.

## 4. Define measures without inventing outcomes

Possible themes include goal completion, channel shift, demand and failure demand, handoff quality, wait time, abandonment, error and rework, accessibility, support need, staff effort, cost, satisfaction, equity, policy outcome, and service reliability. These are prompts, not assigned metrics or targets.

For each measure, record question, accountable owner, formula, numerator, denominator, unit, population, grain, period, channel, filters, exclusions, source, refresh, target, quality limits, privacy constraints, and attribution limits. Reconcile definitions and sources before comparing values. Do not attribute a service outcome to design alone.

## 5. Produce a reviewable blueprint and brief

Include scope, current and proposed states, lanes and legend, evidence references, assumptions, conflicts, pain points, opportunities, dependencies, risks, decision rights, measures, open questions, and implementation or test needs. Add a visible source note naming public guidance, local evidence, dates or versions, and missing evidence.

Stop before participant contact, data or system access, policy or process change, staffing or budget commitment, procurement, supplier instruction, roadmap reprioritization, publication, or external communication. Continue with the [workflow](workflow.md) and [quality check](evaluations/quality-check.md).
