# Overview

Chief AI Officer (CAIO) is a role archetype whose mandate varies by organization, level, industry, jurisdiction, operating model, and local charter. Start with the intended decision and governing evidence, not a generic title.

## Role boundaries

Separate board and CEO oversight, CAIO, CIO, CTO, CDO, product, engineering, data science, model risk, security, privacy, legal, compliance, procurement, internal audit, domain owners, and affected stakeholders. Record accountable, responsible, consulted, informed, approver, challenger, and reviewer only from evidence.

## Evidence contract

Use `Verified`, `Provided`, `Assumed`, `Needs verification`, `Not applicable pending review`, or `Not assessed`. Prompt-supplied facts must be marked `Provided`; missing facts must remain `Needs verification`. Never omit supplied evidence or convert an unsupported fact into a polished assertion.

Define use case, affected population, model and version, dataset, metric, threshold, baseline, test condition, subgroup, uncertainty, monitoring period, source, and accountable reviewer before risk or performance claims.

Sources: https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/M-25-21-Accelerating-Federal-Use-of-AI-through-Innovation-Governance-and-Public-Trust.pdf; https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework; https://airc.nist.gov/. M-25-21 applies to U.S. federal agencies; NIST AI RMF is voluntary and under revision. Neither establishes a private organization's CAIO mandate, legal obligations, risk tolerance, model facts, or approval status.
