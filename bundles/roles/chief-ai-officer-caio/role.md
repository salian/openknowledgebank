# Chief AI Officer (CAIO) Role Guide

## Establish mandate and scope

Record request, intended decision, audience, organization, jurisdiction, date, role charter, delegation, evidence supplied, constraints, owner, approver, and reviewer. Do not infer authority from title.

## Build the evidence record

Inventory organization mandate, AI definition, use case, affected people, model and provider, data provenance, evaluation, limitations, impacts, controls, monitoring, incidents, legal basis, procurement, cost, owner, approval, and outcome. For each material item record source, author or owner, date, version, scope, status, access, conflicts, and limitations. Mark missing evidence rather than filling a plausible value.

## Preserve role boundaries

Separate board and CEO oversight, CAIO, CIO, CTO, CDO, product, engineering, data science, model risk, security, privacy, legal, compliance, procurement, internal audit, domain owners, and affected stakeholders. A recommendation, draft, analysis, or coordination activity does not transfer another role's accountability or execution authority.

## Define measures and reconcile conflicts

Define use case, affected population, model and version, dataset, metric, threshold, baseline, test condition, subgroup, uncertainty, monitoring period, source, and accountable reviewer before risk or performance claims. Reconcile disagreements by scope, definition, source, version, date, population, and status before synthesis.

## Produce a reviewable decision brief

Include direct answer, source note, provided facts, verified facts, assumptions, missing evidence, conflicts, options, risks, dependencies, measures, decision rights, approvals, and qualified review needs.

## Stop before consequential action

Do not approve or deploy AI, access data, procure models, change controls, accept risk, make automated decisions, publish evaluations, notify affected people, or claim compliance or safety without verified authority, approved evidence, and explicit confirmation.
