# CCO / CRO Role Guide

## 1. Establish applicability before mandate

Record jurisdiction, regulator, entity and license, sector, asset or other threshold, parent or subsidiary scope, governing source, effective date, transition, and evidence owner. For each requirement, distinguish mandatory, regulator expectation, contractual, board-approved, internal policy, and advisory guidance.

The investment-adviser designation in 17 CFR 275.206(4)-7 and the covered-bank CRO requirements in 12 CFR 252.33 are useful examples precisely because their scopes differ. Do not transfer either rule to another entity.

## 2. Separate accountability and functions

Map board and committee oversight, chief-executive accountability, business ownership and control execution, compliance and risk oversight and challenge, legal advice, and independent assurance. Record responsible, accountable, consulted, informed, approver, challenger, escalation recipient, and assurance provider only where evidence supports them.

The CCO or CRO may administer a framework and challenge decisions without becoming the owner of every compliance obligation or risk. Internal audit should remain distinct from second-line management responsibilities unless an applicable source explicitly establishes another arrangement.

## 3. Test independence as an operating condition

Review appointment and removal, functional and administrative reporting, board or committee access, agenda rights, private sessions, resources, competence, budget, compensation, performance assessment, conflicts, escalation protection, and documented challenge. A title, organization-chart line, or policy statement alone does not prove independence.

If the roles are combined, assess whether one mandate can suppress the other, whether required independence is preserved, and whether capacity and competence are sufficient. Record mitigations and the approving authority rather than asserting that combination is acceptable.

## 4. Scope authority and access

For each action or information class, record purpose, legal basis, delegation, system, data owner, read or change level, approval, logging, retention, and restrictions. Access may be constrained by privilege, privacy, labor, whistleblower, investigation, bank-secrecy, blocking, confidentiality, security, supervisory, or need-to-know requirements.

Do not convert a governance expectation for access into blanket permission. Do not infer policy approval, veto, risk acceptance, limit change, investigation, filing, certification, regulator-contact, disciplinary, or public-communication authority from role title.

## 5. Preserve evidence and escalation

For each issue, record source, date, scope, criteria, facts, uncertainty, severity basis, owner, challenge, decision, approval, action, deadline, status, residual risk, escalation path, and board or committee treatment. Keep allegation, preliminary assessment, substantiated finding, legal conclusion, management decision, remediation, closure, and recurrence evidence separate.

## 6. Avoid unsupported liability conclusions

Role title alone does not establish supervisory status or personal liability. A defensible review requires applicable law, assigned duties, actual authority and conduct, knowledge, participation, omissions, good-faith actions, documentation, reliance, causation, and forum-specific standards. Preserve legal questions for qualified counsel and do not predict enforcement or individual exposure.

Every deliverable needs a visible source note naming governing sources, effective dates, local artifacts, authority level, assumptions, conflicts, and missing evidence. Continue with the [workflow](workflow.md) and [quality check](evaluations/quality-check.md).
