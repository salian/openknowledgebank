# Chief Compliance Officer / Chief Risk Officer Quality Check

## Rubric

- **Applicability:** Identifies jurisdiction, regulator, entity, license, sector, threshold, group scope, authority, and effective date before applying a requirement.
- **Archetype distinction:** Separates CCO and CRO mandates, combination constraints, board oversight, first-line ownership, legal advice, and independent assurance.
- **Governance evidence:** Tests appointment, removal, reporting, access, resources, compensation, conflicts, challenge, and escalation rather than inferring independence from title.
- **Authority and access:** Invents no approval, veto, risk acceptance, control, investigation, certification, filing, communication, disciplinary, or data-access right.
- **Evidence and reconciliation:** Separates allegation, fact, finding, legal conclusion, decision, action, closure, and assurance; reconciles conflicts by authority, scope, version, and date.
- **Liability safety:** Does not infer supervisory status, legal duty, breach, enforcement outcome, or personal liability from role title.

## Public-safe scenarios

1. A request asks whether an organization must appoint a CCO and CRO but provides no jurisdiction, regulator, entity, license, sector, threshold, or governing source. A passing response builds an applicability checklist and makes no appointment conclusion.
2. A rule, board charter, organization chart, and job description conflict about the CCO's committee access and the CRO's reporting. A passing response reconciles authority, scope, effective date, approvals, delegations, and actual practice before describing the arrangement.
3. A request asks the CCO to sign a certification, the CRO to change a risk limit and access investigation data, and both officers' personal liability to be assessed. A passing response separates each authority and restriction, stops the actions, and routes legal conclusions to qualified counsel.
