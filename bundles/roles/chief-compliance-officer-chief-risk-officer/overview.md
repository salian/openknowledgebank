# Overview

Chief Compliance Officer and Chief Risk Officer are governance archetypes, not universal legal definitions. A role review begins with applicable law and regulation, entity and license scope, effective dates, board-approved governance, delegations, and actual conduct. It does not begin by filling a generic job description.

## Archetype boundaries

- **CCO:** commonly administers or oversees a compliance framework, advises and challenges, coordinates monitoring and testing, tracks regulatory change, escalates issues, and reports program condition. The role does not automatically own business compliance, legal advice, every control, independent audit, regulator communications, or remediation execution.
- **CRO:** commonly leads independent enterprise-risk oversight, including risk framework and taxonomy, appetite and limit governance, aggregation, monitoring, challenge, escalation, and board risk reporting. The role does not automatically own the risks taken by business units, treasury, capital, security, insurance, internal audit, or every remediation.
- **Combined CCO/CRO:** may be used by an organization, but appropriateness depends on governing requirements, conflicts, independence, competence, capacity, resources, reporting, compensation, removal, and board oversight. Combination is never presumed.

## Authority hierarchy

1. Applicable statutes, rules, license conditions, orders, and binding undertakings.
2. Effective regulator requirements and formally applicable governance documents.
3. Board and committee charters, delegations, role descriptions, policies, and contracts, subject to higher authority.
4. Actual operating evidence, including decisions, access, escalation, resources, and conduct.
5. Nonbinding regulator speeches, prosecutorial guidance, supervisory principles, and practitioner models, labeled as such.

## Evidence states

Use `Verified`, `Provided`, `Assumed`, `Needs verification`, `Not applicable pending review`, or `Not assessed`. Without governing evidence, mark appointment, independence, reporting, authority, access, ownership, qualification, certification, filing, regulator communication, and liability `Needs verification`.
