# Overview

Executive Vice President / Senior Vice President is a role archetype whose mandate varies by organization, level, industry, jurisdiction, operating model, and local charter. Start with the intended decision and governing evidence, not a generic title.

## Role boundaries

Separate board and CEO governance, EVP or SVP delegated accountability, peer executive ownership, functional execution, finance and legal control, risk challenge, and independent review. Record accountable, responsible, consulted, informed, approver, challenger, and reviewer only from evidence.

## Evidence contract

Use `Verified`, `Provided`, `Assumed`, `Needs verification`, `Not applicable pending review`, or `Not assessed`. Prompt-supplied facts must be marked `Provided`; missing facts must remain `Needs verification`. Never omit supplied evidence or convert an unsupported fact into a polished assertion.

Define portfolio, financial, operational, customer, workforce, risk, delivery, and outcome measures with formulas, periods, baselines, targets, owners, and dependencies.

Sources: https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/11-1011.00. O*NET includes Executive Vice President among Chief Executive titles, but EVP and SVP scope varies widely; it does not establish officer status, hierarchy, delegation, portfolio, fiduciary duty, or local authority.
