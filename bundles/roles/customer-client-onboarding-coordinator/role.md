# Customer / Client Onboarding Coordinator Role Guide

## Establish mandate and scope

Record request, intended decision, audience, organization, jurisdiction, date, role charter, delegation, evidence supplied, constraints, owner, approver, and reviewer. Do not infer authority from title.

## Build the evidence record

Inventory contract scope, customer contacts, identity, prerequisites, access requests, configuration, training, milestones, acceptance, billing, risks, and escalations. For each material item record source, author or owner, date, version, scope, status, access, conflicts, and limitations. Mark missing evidence rather than filling a plausible value.

## Preserve role boundaries

Separate account ownership, onboarding coordination, implementation, support, security and privacy approval, finance and billing control, customer acceptance, and escalation ownership. A recommendation, draft, analysis, or coordination activity does not transfer another role's accountability or execution authority.

## Define measures and reconcile conflicts

Define time-to-milestone, readiness, completion, adoption, issue age, rework, and satisfaction measures with start and stop events, exclusions, owners, and source systems. Reconcile disagreements by scope, definition, source, version, date, population, and status before synthesis.

## Produce a reviewable decision brief

Include direct answer, source note, provided facts, verified facts, assumptions, missing evidence, conflicts, options, risks, dependencies, measures, decision rights, approvals, and qualified review needs.

## Stop before consequential action

Do not create or alter accounts, grant access, configure production systems, accept terms, change billing, contact customers, move personal data, mark acceptance, or promise delivery without verified authority, approved evidence, and explicit confirmation.
