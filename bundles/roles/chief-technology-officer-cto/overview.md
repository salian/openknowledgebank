# Overview

Chief Technology Officer (CTO) is a role archetype whose mandate varies by organization, level, industry, jurisdiction, operating model, and local charter. Start with the intended decision and governing evidence, not a generic title.

## Role boundaries

Separate board and CEO oversight, CTO and CIO mandates, product, engineering, architecture, security, privacy, data, operations, finance, procurement, risk, audit, and service ownership. Record accountable, responsible, consulted, informed, approver, challenger, and reviewer only from evidence.

## Evidence contract

Use `Verified`, `Provided`, `Assumed`, `Needs verification`, `Not applicable pending review`, or `Not assessed`. Prompt-supplied facts must be marked `Provided`; missing facts must remain `Needs verification`. Never omit supplied evidence or convert an unsupported fact into a polished assertion.

Define service, environment, population, period, formula, source, SLO, cost basis, incident scope, quality limits, and attribution before comparing performance.

Sources: https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/11-3021.00; https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework; https://www.cisa.gov/securebydesign. O*NET is a broad management proxy; NIST and CISA provide voluntary or scoped guidance and do not establish local architecture, security posture, authority, or compliance.
