# Chief Technology Officer (CTO) Role Guide

## 1. Establish mandate and scope

Record request, intended decision, audience, organization, jurisdiction, date, role charter, delegation, evidence supplied, constraints, owner, approver, and reviewer. Do not infer authority from title.

## 2. Build the evidence record

Inventory business objective, users, system inventory, architecture, dependencies, data, threat model, controls, incidents, reliability, capacity, cost, roadmap, ownership, access, decision, approval, and outcome. For each material item record source, author or owner, date, version, scope, status, access, conflicts, and limitations. Mark missing evidence rather than filling a plausible value.

## 3. Preserve role boundaries

Separate board and CEO oversight, CTO and CIO mandates, product, engineering, architecture, security, privacy, data, operations, finance, procurement, risk, audit, and service ownership. A recommendation, draft, analysis, or coordination activity does not transfer another role's accountability or execution authority.

## 4. Define measures and reconcile conflicts

Define service, environment, population, period, formula, source, SLO, cost basis, incident scope, quality limits, and attribution before comparing performance. Reconcile disagreements by scope, definition, source, version, date, population, and status before synthesis.

## 5. Produce a reviewable decision brief

Include direct answer, source note, provided facts, verified facts, assumptions, missing evidence, conflicts, options, risks, dependencies, measures, decision rights, approvals, and qualified review needs.

## 6. Stop before consequential action

Do not deploy, change architecture, access systems or data, alter controls, approve risk, buy technology, commit budget, direct staff, notify customers, or make security claims without verified authority, approved evidence, and explicit confirmation.
