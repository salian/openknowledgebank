# Overview

Use this bundle to organize source-backed Medicare participation and coverage requirement reviews without treating every provider or supplier as subject to the same conditions.

## What This Bundle Is For

- Identify the provider/supplier type, certified entity, locations/components, services, program, and survey pathway.
- Resolve the governing CFR part, subpart, and section and preserve its actual regulatory label and scope.
- Determine which rule text applied on the relevant date, including amendment timing.
- Connect applicable requirements to reviewable evidence, gaps, owners, and accountable decisions.

## Provider-Type Boundary

CMS identifies many organization types subject to CoPs or CfCs, while 42 CFR Chapter IV distributes their requirements across multiple parts and subchapters. Parts may also contain payment provisions, state-agency requirements, or several provider-specific subparts. A title, part number, checklist, tag, or rule from one provider type is not automatically applicable to another.

## Current and Effective Rule Boundary

Record the eCFR currency date and inspect historical text for the event under review. For amendments, distinguish publication, effective, applicability, compliance, and implementation dates. Label proposed, delayed, expired, superseded, or future-effective material accurately; do not present it as the operative requirement.

## What This Bundle Is Not For

- It is not legal, clinical, accreditation, survey, certification, enforcement, reimbursement, or state-licensure advice.
- It does not replace current eCFR text, Federal Register amendments, CMS provider pages, the applicable State Operations Manual appendix, CMS memoranda, state law, or qualified review.
- It does not assign survey findings, scope/severity, immediate jeopardy, corrective action, deemed status, certification, or enforcement consequences.

## Risk Boundary

Protect sensitive clinical, resident/patient, personnel, quality, survey, complaint, accreditation, legal, and security records. Require explicit authorization and qualified review before any external communication, plan of correction, certification, disclosure, or operational change.
