# Medicare Participation Requirement Review Workflow

## 1. Define the Question

Record the decision sought, provider/supplier type, program, entity, certification scope, locations/components, service, event date, review period, survey pathway, audience, and accountable reviewer. Mark unresolved facts `Needs verification`.

Do not begin with a generic checklist or assume “Medicare participation” means the same thing as accepting assignment for physician/supplier claims.

## 2. Resolve the Governing Source Set

Identify the exact CFR part, provider-specific subpart, section, and definition. Add Parts 488 and 489 only where survey, certification, enforcement, agreement, approval, or effective-date process requires them. Identify applicable state/local, Medicaid, accreditation/deeming, CMS memorandum, and organization layers separately.

Record why each source applies. Reject cross-provider borrowing unless an authoritative cross-reference supports it.

## 3. Establish Point-in-Time Currency

Capture the current eCFR currency date and current text. Use point-in-time history for the event date. Inspect the Federal Register amendment when timing matters and distinguish publication, effective, applicability, compliance, and implementation dates.

Classify each source as current, historical, proposed, delayed, expired, superseded, stayed, waived, future-effective, or `Needs verification`. Do not merge these states.

## 4. Build the Requirement Matrix

For each requirement, record citation, version, provider/location/service scope, date range, regulatory label, incorporated source, responsible function, required evidence, reviewer, and unresolved interpretation.

Separate regulation from CMS interpretive guidance, survey procedure, accreditation standard, state law, and internal policy.

## 5. Inventory and Protect Evidence

List the evidence population, period, owner, system/source, access authority, retention constraints, and sampling approach. Use redacted or aggregated evidence where possible and maintain minimum-necessary access.

Do not move protected records into public or unapproved systems. Record inaccessible evidence as a gap.

## 6. Test Without Overclaiming

Compare authorized evidence to the applicable requirement and approved acceptance criterion. Distinguish design from implementation and current operation. Record contradictory, stale, partial, or missing evidence explicitly.

Use only the permitted result statuses. Do not call a gap a deficiency or infer compliance from a policy, accreditation certificate, isolated sample, or absence of a known citation.

## 7. Triage and Escalate

Classify the issue as source/applicability, evidence, policy/design, implementation, monitoring, survey/certification, or legal/clinical interpretation. Assign an owner and qualified reviewer.

Escalate potential patient/resident harm, immediate-jeopardy concerns, reportable events, active surveys, official deadlines, certification risk, enforcement, or appeals immediately through authorized channels. Do not recommend or execute external contact, disclosure, plan of correction, operational change, or certification without explicit authority.

## 8. Reconcile the Direct Answer

State:

1. the provider/supplier and certification scope actually reviewed;
2. the exact current or point-in-time authority and its date status;
3. evidence verified, evidence only provided, assumptions, and needs-verification items;
4. the narrow answer supported by that evidence;
5. unresolved gaps and accountable next review;
6. actions that remain unauthorized.

If the source or provider scope is unresolved, the direct answer must say that no applicability or compliance conclusion can be made.

## 9. Monitor Change

Track eCFR amendments, CMS transmittals and memoranda, provider-specific manual appendices, state requirements, deeming-program status, certification scope, services, locations/components, ownership, and organization controls. Revalidate when any trigger changes; do not rely on a static checklist as proof of current compliance.
