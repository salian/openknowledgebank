# Section 508 Review Guide

## 1. Establish legal and acquisition context

Record the federal department or agency, ICT item and components, development/procurement/maintenance/use activity, employee and public users, lifecycle dates, acquisition vehicle, requiring activity, agency policy, and contract terms. Section 508 directly addresses covered federal agencies; a private vendor's duties depend on the federal acquisition and contract context and other applicable law.

Do not use Section 508 as a synonym for the ADA, WCAG generally, or every accessibility obligation. Record each authority separately.

## 2. Map the Revised 508 Standards

Inventory web content, non-Web electronic documents, software and platform content, hardware, support documentation and services, authoring tools, and mixed ICT. Map each component to 508 Chapters 1 and 2 and the relevant provisions in Chapters 3 through 7.

E205 applies WCAG 2.0 Level A and AA success criteria and conformance requirements to public-facing electronic content and specified categories of non-public agency official communications, subject to stated non-Web exceptions and substitutions. E207 applies WCAG 2.0 A and AA to software user-interface components and content, with its stated exceptions, alongside chapter 5.

WCAG does not replace the hardware, support-documentation, functional-performance, interoperability, and other applicable 508 requirements. WCAG 2.1 or 2.2 may be an agency, contract, or good-practice requirement, but the Revised 508 Standards incorporate WCAG 2.0. Never silently change the legal baseline.

## 3. Review exceptions and alternatives

Distinguish legacy safe harbor, national security systems, contractor-incidental ICT, maintenance or monitoring spaces, undue burden, fundamental alteration, best meets, and equivalent facilitation. Each has different scope, decision authority, evidence, and consequences.

Do not infer an exception from cost, age, commercial availability, or a vendor statement. Record the responsible agency determination and required documentation. Where the governing provision requires alternative access, record the identified user need and evidence without assuming that the alternative cures every issue.

## 4. Assess conformance evidence

An Accessibility Conformance Report is a scoped statement about a product and standards edition. A VPAT is a template used to create an ACR; neither is government certification or conclusive proof. Record the exact product/version, configuration, report date, author, criteria addressed, conformance terms, remarks, exclusions, evaluation methods, and known defects.

Reconcile ACR assertions with contract requirements, test results, user scenarios, assistive-technology evidence, defect records, and version changes. Neither source is automatically right until scope, environment, method, and criteria align. Automated checks alone do not establish full WCAG or Section 508 conformance.

For a WCAG conformance claim, preserve the claimed edition, level, pages or product scope, complete processes, relied-upon technologies, test date, and limitations. Do not convert a sampled pass percentage into an unqualified conformance claim.

## 5. Trace the acquisition lifecycle

FAR subpart 39.2 requires applicable accessibility standards in covered federal ICT acquisitions unless a documented exception or exemption applies. Trace user needs, component inventory, applicable provisions, market research, solicitation requirements, offeror response, evaluation, contract terms, testing, acceptance, maintenance, and changes.

Keep legal compliance, technical conformance, procurement acceptability, actual accessibility, and remediation status as separate conclusions. Escalate uncertain coverage, mixed ICT, exception claims, standard-version conflicts, incomplete ACRs, test disagreement, and acceptance issues to accountable agency legal, acquisition, Section 508, and accessibility reviewers.
