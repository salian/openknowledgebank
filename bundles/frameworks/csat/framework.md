# Customer Satisfaction Score Source-Aware Guide

Source-aware framework bundle for survey question, response scale, satisfied threshold, sampling, timing, response rate, score, segmentation, and interpretation review, evidence reconciliation, reviewable decisions, and controlled consequential actions.

Apply this guidance as a decision aid, not as proof of local facts, outcomes, compliance, professional judgment, or authorization.

## Authoritative and Identified Sources
- https://www.qualtrics.com/articles/customer-experience/what-is-csat/
- https://theacsi.org/company/the-acsi-difference/

Name an applicable URL in every Source Note. Verify current version, date, scope, and applicability. Do not reproduce licensed standards or proprietary methods; disclose when a source is secondary or a licensed primary text is still required.

## Evidence Required
- survey purpose and exact question, scale and satisfied threshold, invitation population and sampling, channel and timing, response counts and exclusions, score formula, weighting, segments, period, confidence or uncertainty treatment, consent and privacy, benchmarks and comparability, source extract, owner, and approvals

## Application Sequence
1. Define the decision, scope, owner, date, and source version.
2. Inventory evidence as verified, provided, assumed, or needing verification.
3. Apply only source-supported concepts to inspected local evidence.
4. Reconcile definitions, identifiers, versions, periods, scope, permissions, processing, and ownership.
5. Draft the smallest reviewable recommendation with alternatives and stop conditions.
6. Obtain accountable confirmation before consequential action.

## Guardrails
- Do not infer customer sentiment, representativeness, score formula, benchmark comparability, causal driver, trend significance, or business outcome.
- Do not invent artifact provenance, access, execution, approval, or reviewer ownership.
- Require accountable confirmation before actions that send surveys, contact customers, collect or export personal data, change scoring or dashboards, publish scores or benchmarks, trigger outreach, or claim causal improvement.
