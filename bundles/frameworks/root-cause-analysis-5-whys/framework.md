# Root Cause Analysis and Five Whys Source-Aware Guide

Source-aware framework bundle for problem statement, causal chain, evidence, contributing causes, verification, corrective action, and learning review, evidence reconciliation, reviewable decisions, and controlled consequential actions.

Apply this guidance as a decision aid, not as proof of local facts, outcomes, compliance, professional judgment, or authorization.

## Authoritative and Identified Sources
- https://asq.org/quality-resources/root-cause-analysis
- https://asq.org/quality-resources/five-whys

Name an applicable URL in every Source Note. Verify current version, date, scope, and applicability. Do not reproduce licensed standards or proprietary methods; disclose when a source is secondary or a licensed primary text is still required.

## Evidence Required
- event and impact definition, timeline, affected scope, logs and observations, process and system changes, causal hypotheses, each why-answer and supporting evidence, alternative causes, human factors, experiments or recurrence tests, corrective and preventive actions, owners, dates, approvals, and follow-up results

## Application Sequence
1. Define the decision, scope, owner, date, and source version.
2. Inventory evidence as verified, provided, assumed, or needing verification.
3. Apply only source-supported concepts to inspected local evidence.
4. Reconcile definitions, identifiers, versions, periods, scope, permissions, processing, and ownership.
5. Draft the smallest reviewable recommendation with alternatives and stop conditions.
6. Obtain accountable confirmation before consequential action.

## Guardrails
- Do not infer root cause, causal link, fault, recurrence risk, corrective-action effectiveness, closure, or accountability.
- Do not invent artifact provenance, access, execution, approval, or reviewer ownership.
- Require accountable confirmation before actions that assign blame or root cause, change production processes or controls, stop operations, close incidents or corrective actions, communicate findings, or make safety, legal, or disciplinary determinations.
