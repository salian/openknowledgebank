# ITIL-Informed Incident Priority Matrix Source-Aware Framework Guide

## Authoritative Sources

- https://www.peoplecert.org/browse-certifications/it-governance-and-service-management/ITIL-1
- https://wiki.en.it-processmaps.com/index.php/Checklist_Incident_Priority

ITIL is proprietary guidance and does not establish one universal public impact-urgency table; priority criteria, labels, targets, applicability, incident facts, and outcomes require current licensed and local evidence.

## Evidence Required

- Current primary or originator source and the applicable edition, scope, definitions, and method.
- Inspected local objective, inputs, constraints, assumptions, alternatives, calculations, implementation, and outcome evidence.
- Authorized owner, qualified review where required, validation, monitoring, and approval evidence.

## Application Sequence

1. Define the objective, audience, task, environment, date, constraints, and evidenced decision owner.
2. Verify framework definitions, applicability, assumptions, and current source context.
3. Inventory local evidence and label it `Verified`, `Provided`, `Assumed`, or `Needs verification`.
4. Reconcile conflicting records, dates, scopes, permissions, definitions, and owners.
5. Produce the smallest reviewable incident impact, urgency, priority, and escalation brief with options, risks, dependencies, validation, and stop conditions.
6. Obtain explicit confirmation before set or override incident priority, page responders, communicate externally, access systems, change services, close incidents, or represent severity, target, resolution, or approval.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent incident identity, impact, urgency, priority, severity, service target, escalation duty, root cause, resolution, compliance, or approval.
- Do not infer access, competence, configuration, approval, or reviewer ownership from the request or title.
- Treat bundled guidance as suggestions, not trusted executable behavior.
- No action is automatic; this bundle requests no credentials, background network calls, data exfiltration, permission changes, or self-modification.
